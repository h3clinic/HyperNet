# HyperNet v0.4 research synthesis: why training hurts on Gravity Spy, and what to change

Written for the student and for an expert reviewer. Every number below is either from the repo's frozen benchmark READMEs or from a source that was fetched and checked in this research pass; anything else is marked unverified.

## 1. What we know (verified)

**The result to explain.** Under frozen protocols on Gravity Spy v1.1 TEST (1,179 spectrograms, 20 types, chance 0.049), the trained 755k-parameter CNN's final embedding scores 0.219 mean AP; the same CNN at random weights scores 0.388; raw 48x48 pixels score 0.485 [file:///C:/Users/brita/Documents/Codex/2026-11-01/wan/public/HyperNet/benchmarks/gravity_spy_v0.1/README.md]. Reading early layers of the random network gives 0.559 [.../gravity_spy_v0.2/README.md]. Retraining with early blocks locked at their random start, the trained coarse block never beats the same block at random (TEST differences -0.004 to -0.029, all intervals crossing zero); the best four-layer readout reaches 0.572 but the all-random four-layer mix scores 0.576 [.../gravity_spy_v0.3/README.md]. The DEV diagnosis is per layer: stem +0.004, fine -0.121, middle -0.199, coarse -0.258 AP against the same random start, mostly inside epoch 1, in all three curricula.

**What the code actually does.** The loss (`matching_loss_v2`) is symmetric InfoNCE at temperature 0.1 over in-batch index targets for both the 128-d `global` embedding and a Sinkhorn-matched `local` score, plus a mask loss, a four-head "grammar" classifier on the raw embedding, and VICReg-style variance/covariance terms weighted 5 and 1. The positive pair is two renders of one object program. The input stack is fixed (RGB, local contrast, signed Sobel, edge magnitude). On the benchmark side, `to_uint8_rgb` takes a single scalar from trainingsetv1d1.h5 and replicates it to three channels, so the RGB gain in `reference_adapter.positive_views` acts as a brightness gain only, and half of the 16 reference views are time-flipped and rotated by up to 12 degrees [file:///C:/Users/brita/Documents/Codex/2026-11-01/wan/public/HyperNet/hypernet/reference_adapter.py].

**Random features are a recognised strong baseline, not a fluke.** Certain convolutional pooling architectures are inherently frequency selective and translation invariant with random weights, and a large fraction of trained performance is attributable to architecture alone [https://mlanthology.org/icml/2011/saxe2011icml-random]. On audio spectrograms, random VGG features with an SVM beat MFCCs on all three datasets tested (GTZAN 59.65% vs 53.44%; Extended Ballroom 89.82% vs 63.25%; UrbanSound8K 70.74% vs 65.49%) while trailing trained CNNs (82.1%, 93.7%, 73%) [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.00237]. Saxe averaged 10 random weight sets per architecture on NORB and 5 on CIFAR-10 [https://icml.cc/2011/papers/551_icmlpaper.pdf]; HyperNet's random control is one draw (seed 1403), and its seed-to-seed spread has never been measured.

**No published synthetic pretraining loses to random init, but none measures HyperNet's metric.** Under a shared ResNet-50/MoCo-v2 protocol every synthetic dataset beats random init on ImageNet-1k linear probing (random 4.36, Dead-leaves 20.00, FractalDB-1k 23.86, Shaders-21k 30.25, Shaders-21k+MixUp 44.83) [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.16412]; with AlexNet on ImageNet-100, random CNN 0.21 vs Dead-leaves Squares 0.35, Shapes 0.39, Textures 0.41, StyleGAN-Oriented 0.43, real 0.63 [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.05963]. All of these use thousands of real labels in a linear probe; none evaluates frozen cosine retrieval from one reference. HyperNet's trained-worse-than-random result is therefore anomalous relative to this literature, which points at HyperNet's objective and data rather than at synthetic pretraining as such.

**No published Gravity Spy one-reference mAP exists.** The closest is Coughlin et al. 2019: the DIRECT embedding (ImageNet VGG16 plus a contrastive pair loss on labeled pairs, 200-d, cosine) discards about 99.0% of the archive at 50% retention of Water Jet, where raw pixels and PCA reach only about 30.0%; on Raven Peck all three methods reach about 99% [https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04058v2]. DIRECT needs class labels, which HyperNet's rules forbid. Everything else is supervised classification (Zevin 2017: 97.1% on 20 classes [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.04596]; Bahaadini merged view 96.89% [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.00034]; Wu et al. 0.941 accuracy on O3 [https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12913v2]) or clustering (CTSAE ARI 0.4091 / NMI 0.6362, unsupervised, no augmentation [https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15552v1]). The 2026 OmegaNeuron paper uses the frozen DIRECT embedding with cosine similarity and no threshold [https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23460v1]; it is newer than my training data and provides no runnable baseline. A fixed extractor applied to v1.1.0 exists: wavelet scattering with a random forest reaches 88.57% vs 87.41% for the Q-transform image and 91.03% merged [https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19122v2] (2025, read from arXiv HTML only).

**Four things the critic flagged that change how the numbers should be read.** (1) The "about 90 false alarms per 10,000 for every method" is the 1% DEV cutoff by construction, and TEST v1.1 (about 1,100 negatives per target) cannot certify any rate below roughly 25 per 10,000 by the rule of three [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(statistics)]. (2) Raw pixels score 0.379 with the 600-image per-feature standardization and 0.485 without; the same standardizer is applied to every CNN readout, so every CNN-vs-pixels comparison depends on an unexamined normalization choice; on DEV, per-pixel standard deviation varies about 15x across frequency rows (DEV-only re-scoring, not published). (3) The v15 experiment drew its "fresh" 4,044 events from previously unexposed v1.1 rows, so essentially all of v1.1 has been scored under some protocol. (4) The -0.004 to -0.029 trained-minus-random differences come from one random seed and one training seed per curriculum.

## 2. Why HyperNet training hurts (mechanism and evidence)

The verified literature gives a consistent mechanism with four parts. The first two are the likely root cause; the last two explain the depth profile.

**(a) The positive pair is solvable by a shortcut, so the harder features are never learned and existing ones are pruned.** Under contrastive loss, a few easy-to-learn bits shared by the two views suppress, and can fully prevent, learning of other features, and this does not happen under supervised loss [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.02803]. InfoNCE encoders favor the simpler of two available features, and lower contrastive loss can mean worse accuracy on the harder feature; which pairs are presented decides which features become discriminative [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.11230]. Two renders of one exact program share exact geometry, so identity is cheap; nothing in the pair forces the network to keep the texture, blob and streak statistics that spectrogram types are made of. The v15 scope review reached the same conclusion from the task side: training pairs are two views of one program while the task needs invariance across independent examples of a type [file:///C:/Users/brita/Documents/Codex/2026-11-01/wan/public/HyperNet/experiments/v15/benchmarks/gravity_spy/provenance/experiments/v15_objective_scope_review.json]. Using positives drawn from other instances raised ImageNet linear accuracy from 71.7% to 75.6% and cut augmentation dependence to a 2.1% drop with crops only [https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14548].

**(b) Contrastive models become invariant to exactly their augmentations, and HyperNet's augmentations destroy spectrogram identity.** Learned invariances strongly affect downstream performance and different tasks need opposite invariances [https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11398]. HyperNet's reference views include time flips, rotations of up to 12 degrees (mixing the time and frequency axes) and per-channel gains [reference_adapter.py]. In the one controlled spectrogram-image study found, vertical flips and color filters lowered PR AUC below the no-augmentation baseline (0.741 and 0.754 vs 0.763) while time flips helped (0.819) [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9421122/]. Standard 2D convolution already wrongly enforces translation equivariance along frequency [https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15296], and adding a frequency-position encoding with 515 parameters matched an 8M-parameter alternative [https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13252]. Gravity Spy class morphologies are defined by absolute frequency and duration (Blip about 0.04 s broadband; Tomte 16-150 Hz; Power Line at 60 Hz harmonics; 1080 Lines; Whistle above 128 Hz) [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.12849]. The CTSAE authors avoid augmentation on glitch spectrograms for exactly this reason [https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15552v1].

**(c) The damage is early and grows with depth, as the critical-period and tunnel-effect literature predicts.** Fisher information between layers rises in the first epochs and strong connections formed early do not change later; the initial transient largely determines the outcome [https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08856]. In deep networks the later layers compress representations, degrade out-of-distribution probes, appear early in training, and OOD probe performance tracks representation rank; the best place to probe external data is the last layer before the tunnel [https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19753]. Compression is worst for low-diversity, few-class data and shrinks with many classes and higher resolution [https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15018]. Contrastive embeddings also collapse to a low-dimensional subspace, driven by strong augmentation and over-parameterized projectors; removing the trainable projector and feeding a subvector of the representation to the loss (DirectCLR) outperforms SimCLR with a linear projector [https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09348]. On a small ResNet-18 from scratch, the VICReg variance/covariance family is the most augmentation-sensitive SSL method (18.1% vs 37.5% top-1 weak vs strong augmentation) [https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02903]. First-layer features are generic across tasks [https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1792], which is why HyperNet's stem survives. This is a plausible mechanism, not a measured one: no source measures per-layer rank at epoch 0 vs epoch 1 on an out-of-domain target.

**(d) The synthetic distribution is the wrong texture class.** Diversity (recall, r=0.83) and natural color statistics (r=-0.57 for color distance) predicted transfer; flat-shape generators transferred worst and oriented, textured ones best; there is a sweet spot of perceptual variation between positives around 0.37 [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.05963]. Transfer scales log-linearly with the number of distinct programs and the worst programs "collapsed to repetitive shapes, grayscale, or solid color" [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.16412]. Category count matters more than instances per category, and contours must be dense [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.08515]; breaking contours destroys the pretraining signal [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09132]; contour variety is crucial [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01112]. The audio FDSL line builds spectrogram-like data from harmonic stacks, frequency modulation, envelopes, filtered noise and background noise and trains a 1.1M-parameter CRNN from PSDS1 0.352 to 0.405 [https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04428] (2025); the 2026 AudioPG and FormulaBank papers (adding damping plus noise gives the biggest jump; procedural audio prefers 10-25% mask ratios) are unverified beyond their arXiv HTML [https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14791], [https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15067v1].

**The honest bottom line.** No published result shows synthetic-only pretrained features beating the same architecture at random initialization under frozen one-reference retrieval on spectrograms. The evidence above predicts that the failure comes from the pair definition, the axis-mixing views and the flat data, and that fixing those should stop training from removing glitch information. Whether it goes further and beats random is HyperNet's experiment to run.

## 3. Ranked v0.4 design candidates

Ranked by expected effect divided by one-CPU cost. The structured list carries the exact changes; the summary here is for reading order. Candidates 1 and 3 are the two experimental factors and must be run as a 2x2 (old data/new objective, new data/old objective, both, neither) or no gain can be attributed to either. Candidate 4 is a prerequisite that should be executed first even though it ranks fourth on effect, because it fixes the bar everything else is measured against.

1. **Family-member positives with a many-positive objective.** A type becomes a parameter distribution; each positive is an independently sampled member; the loss becomes SupCon or cross-entropy over thousands of family labels; the variance/covariance term is dropped. This attacks mechanism (a) directly.
2. **Axis-respecting views and inputs.** Remove rotations, vertical geometry and per-channel gains from positive views; keep time shifts, time/frequency masking and band gain; add a fixed frequency-coordinate channel; compare at matching frequency rows. Attacks (b). Near-zero cost.
3. **Spectrogram-like synthetic distribution, chosen by a label-free coverage diagnostic.** 1/f or colored-noise backgrounds carrying Gaussian blobs, chirped streaks, periodic bands, broadband bursts and repeated events at varied SNR, thousands of families; pick the generator whose random-CNN features best cover the 600 declared unlabeled spectrograms before any training run. Attacks (d).
4. **Measurement prerequisites.** Per-arm normalization frozen in protocol.json, 5-10 random seeds and a width sweep for the control, label-free fusion of per-image-z pixels with the random early mix as the new bar (0.597 DEV, unpublished), blank-reference rule.
5. **Rank preservation.** Apply the loss to a subvector of the pooled coarse features (no 256-unit projector), keep the early-layer readout, and count classes in the thousands. Attacks (c).
6. **In-domain unlabeled pretraining as a declared separate budget.** Masked-reconstruction or BYOL-A-style training on tens of thousands of O3 spectrograms disjoint from every test set. Highest documented effect in the literature, but it changes the data budget and must be reported as its own arm.
7. **Physics injection families rendered through the Gravity Spy pipeline.** Sine-Gaussians, ring-downs, white-noise bursts, scattered-light arches, line harmonics, blip trains and chirps in colored Gaussian noise from an analytic aLIGO PSD. The most literal match to the class definitions, with an unmeasured sim-to-real gap.

## 4. Fresh test data plan

Use Gravity Spy O3 rows from Zenodo 5649212 (CC BY 4.0; eight CSVs with event_time, peak_time, snr, gravityspy_id, 22 per-class confidences, ml_label, ml_confidence, url1-url4) [https://zenodo.org/records/5649212]. v1.1 (2018) predates O3, so O3a/O3b rows are disjoint from everything HyperNet has scored; confirm by matching gravityspy_id against the v1.1 metadata (not yet done). Select candidates with ml_confidence >= 0.95 whose volunteer consensus label in Zenodo 5911227 (retired flag, >0.9 posterior) agrees with the ML label [https://zenodo.org/records/5911227]. Cluster rows by event_time before sampling so near-simultaneous triggers and the four durations of one glitch stay in one group. Download with gwpy GravitySpyTable.download(download_durs=(0.5,1.0,2.0,4.0)) [https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gwpy/gwpy/main/gwpy/table/gravityspy.py], in small batches as the Zenodo page asks. Apply the v1.1 crop x=[66,532], y=[105,671] [https://zenodo.org/records/1486046] and convert the viridis PNG to one scalar; the exact map from PNG to the h5 scalar is unverified (the blank floor 0.0832 is close to viridis' darkest luma, which suggests luma, but this is inference), so declare the fresh set as a new protocol, not a reproduction. Target about 200 events per type per detector where available; Chirp (8+12 at p>0.9) and Wandering Line (10+28) cannot support per-class claims and are reported with intervals or dropped [https://arxiv.org/html/2208.12849v2]. O3 has 23 classes (adds Fast_Scattering and Blip_Low_Frequency, drops None_of_the_Above), so remap the 20-type list in writing before scoring. Keep the remaining roughly 300k O3 rows as a hidden-label haystack. Hash protocol.json (date, references, readouts, normalization, selection rule, bootstrap unit) before any TEST scoring, and report the old-vs-fresh gap as a result in itself, since new test sets shift scores mainly through distribution shift rather than adaptivity [https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10811]. No O4 image or label release exists on Zenodo; GWOSC O4a strain could feed a discovery haystack but not a scored benchmark.

## 5. False-alarm plan

First restate the problem: the 1% DEV cutoff yields about 100 per 10,000 by construction, and TEST v1.1 cannot certify below about 25 per 10,000. A one-in-10,000 operating point needs the O3 haystack (100k negatives bound the rate at 0.3 per 10,000 with zero observed). Then add label-free score corrections, each tuned on DEV only and frozen: (a) adaptive cohort normalization of each reference-by-archive cosine against the top-N most similar unlabeled archive items on both sides (about 20% relative EER/minDCF gain with an in-domain cohort; a mismatched cohort is worse than none) [https://arxiv.org/html/2007.07689v2]; (b) hubness correction by Mutual Proximity, which cut hub skewness from 2.5 to 0.29 across 30 datasets with no significant loss [https://jmlr.org/papers/volume13/schnitzer12a/schnitzer12a.pdf], or k-reciprocal re-ranking, whose gain depends on several true matches per query (Market-1501 mAP 46.0 to 59.9; none on single-shot CUHK03) [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.08398]; (c) fusion of the four durations, which is free in the one-reference protocol and gave 92.9-95.3% single-view to 96.9% merged [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.00034] and 0.911 to 0.941 with attention fusion [https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12913v2]; (d) a per-reference tail threshold from a generalized Pareto fit to impostor scores (SPOT) instead of a global cutoff [https://www.eecs.yorku.ca/course_archive/2018-19/F/6412/reading/kdd17p1067.pdf]. Expect 20-40% relative reductions per method; no source documents a 10x drop from these tools alone; only cascades with a second independent verifier reach that [https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse576/21sp/notes/11_FaceDetRec_21.pdf]. Report false alarms per 10,000 with a binomial interval and the count of negatives behind it.

## 6. Evaluation changes

Multi-seed random control (at least 5 seeds, plus a width sweep); per-arm normalization frozen before TEST; the 2x2 factorial for attribution; a label-free generator diagnostic before training; event-level and class-level paired bootstrap with time-group and duration-group clusters (paired tests raised conclusive few-shot comparisons from 42% to 70% [https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02850]); mean AP plus recall at a stated false-alarm count, never ROC alone [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4349800]; fixed-extractor arms (Kymatio Scattering2D [https://www.kymat.io/codereference.html], raw pixels, the fusion bar); a written blank-image rule (139 constant-floor images, two blank frozen references); a statement of every use of TEST v1.1 and what was selected on it; and a one-shot confirmatory run on the fresh O3 set. Frame the negative result as a diagnosed mechanism; the ISEF rubric scores reproducibility, statistics and interpretation of limitations, not whether the hypothesis held [https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/].

## 7. Open risks

The minimum detectable paired effect with 20 classes is roughly 0.015-0.045 mAP (my back-calculation from v0.3 interval widths, unpublished), so small true effects will stay invisible. The sim-to-real gap of injection families is unmeasured; no paper trains on injections and tests on Gravity Spy images [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.09933], [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.03867]. In-domain SSL on roughly 10^4 images is near the lower edge where it reliably helps, and that threshold is assembled from fine-tuning studies, not frozen one-shot readouts. Regenerated or fresh-downloaded spectrograms are not pixel-identical to the h5 arrays. Several 2025-2026 sources (Licciardi, Formula-SED, AudioPG, FormulaBank, OmegaNeuron, Cirfeta) were read from arXiv HTML only. And the most likely outcome is still that a well-designed v0.4 ties random rather than beating it; the protocol must make that result publishable.
