What the mathematics contributes—and what it does not

The implemented experiment combines support-derived variation tolerance, bounded foreground weights and partial one-to-one part matching. It asks whether coherent evidence can reduce accidental background matches while retaining one training image per reference and no pretrained weights.

This review combines sixteen recent-source entries and the established GrabCut ingredient. It describes the inspected implementation, without recognition metrics or a superiority claim. A recent publication date is distinguished from an earlier preprint where verified. Mathematical ideas and reported model accuracy carry different training budgets.

The mathematics actually implemented

Each reference and candidate window produces fourteen 64-dimensional normalized descriptors: one whole-region tile, four 2×2 tiles and nine 3×3 tiles. Horizontal reference flips are allowed. The current CNN is already trained before these statistics are computed; this stage performs no weight updates.

For each orientation and part, the original descriptor and sixteen Gaussian shape_pattern variants provide seventeen observations. These variants independently deform canonical 32-pixel tiles; they are not coherent deformations of the complete object. The mean is normalized. Per-feature population variance uses the unnormalized sample mean:

μᵢ = normalize(meanₖ zᵢₖ),   vᵢd = meanₖ (zᵢₖd − meanₖ zᵢₖd)²
cᵢd = 0.5 vᵢd + 0.5 mean_d(vᵢd) + 0.01
pᵢd = clip[(1/cᵢd) / mean_d(1/cᵢd), 0.25, 4]
sᵢj = 0.5 cos(originalᵢ, queryⱼ)
     + 0.5 (μᵢᵀ diag(pᵢ) queryⱼ) / (‖μᵢ‖pᵢ ‖queryⱼ‖pᵢ)

This is precision-weighted cosine, not Mahalanobis distance. Clipping follows mean normalization, so the final precision need not have mean exactly one. The diagonal statistic describes sensitivity to specified synthetic transformations; it does not estimate variation across category members. Mahalanobis++ motivates careful feature geometry, but its pretrained, many-example class statistics are not reproduced.

GrabCut runs for five iterations on the 96×96 support. Two-pixel corner seeds are definite background; a six-pixel border is probable background; the central 10×10 square is definite foreground. Other pixels begin as probable foreground. Foreground area outside [0.15, 0.95], or a solver exception, triggers an all-foreground fallback. Tile weight combines foreground fraction and grayscale contrast, has a 0.1 floor and maximum ratio four, and is normalized. Uniform references abstain. These automatic seeds are assumptions; the original GrabCut paper (2004) uses user interaction. The returned mask is not ground truth.

Each grid level solves the following exact linear assignment using zero-gain dummy choices. Weights sum to one within that level:

maximize Σᵢⱼ Pᵢⱼ wᵢ(sᵢⱼ − 0.2 − gᵢⱼ)
Pᵢⱼ ∈ {0,1}; each row and column sum ≤ 1
gᵢⱼ = 0.12 × Manhattan grid distance when distance ≤ 1; otherwise 2
coverage = Σmatched i wᵢ
level score = Σpositive selected gains / 0.8, or zero if coverage < 0.6
region score R = 0.25 max(0,s₀₀) + 0.25 coarse score + 0.5 fine score

Both levels must pass coverage. Unmatched mass is never removed from the denominator. Crucially, coverage is a post-solve rejection gate: the solver can reject a high-gain assignment even if another feasible assignment would meet coverage. It does not optimize under a minimum-coverage constraint. This adapts partial assignment ideas from OPGM; it is neither that trained graph matcher nor a Gromov–Wasserstein solver.

Four fixed support-part permutations preserve the whole tile and permute each grid separately. For each region and orientation, the implementation computes:

C(r,o) = Rintact(r,o) × [Rintact(r,o) − mean_b Rshuffle,b(r,o)]
final score = max over regions r and orientations o of C(r,o)

The signed margin is retained. This is per-region contrast followed by maximization, not a difference between independently maximized scores. Four shuffles are neither an exhaustive null nor exchangeable draws under a proven statistical hypothesis. The result is an empirical score, not a probability, p-value or false-positive guarantee. The same complete scoring procedure must be calibrated on development data.

Research evidence and adoption boundaries

Primary source and chronologyUseful mathematics, data budget and current decision
Distortable CanvasJournal: July 2025. First preprint: January 2022.Bounded deformation matching without pretraining, demonstrated with one to ten examples on characters and sketches. Natural-photo abstraction remains unresolved. Borrow deformation discipline; do not transfer its abstract-image accuracy claims.
Mahalanobis++May 2025 preprint; ICML 2025.Normalization improves Gaussian-distance OOD scoring across 44 pretrained ImageNet models. HyperNet already normalizes descriptors. The adopted bounded precision is a separate engineering adaptation.
Augmentation Invariant Manifold Learning2025 revision/JRSSB; first preprint November 2022.Product-manifold theory separates structure and nuisance. Experiments use dataset-scale MNIST, STL-10 and ImageNet learning. One support explores one nuisance fiber, not the distribution of different category members.
LeJEPANovember 2025 preprint examined.Characteristic-function regularization discourages collapse through Gaussian projections. Dataset-scale representation training precedes downstream low-shot tests. Not implemented: unit-normalized HyperNet descriptors cannot follow a full Euclidean Gaussian.
KerJEPADecember 2025 preprint examined.MMD/KSD generalize distribution regularization. Principal evidence trains ViT-S/8 on ImageNette for 800 epochs. Useful theoretical alternatives, but neither loss nor published trained weights are used here.
FACTORMay 2026 preprint examined.Appearance counterfactuals adjust frozen Grounding DINO predictions on corrupted detection benchmarks. Its Swin/BERT pretraining violates our budget. HyperNet’s structural shuffles are an analogy, not a reproduction.
Recurrent explaining-awayJanuary 2026 journal publication.Occluder information can help interpret missing object features. Experiments include 50,000 composites from 24,000 FashionMNIST images and fine-tuned CORnet. Partial matching borrows an intuition; no recurrent biological mechanism is implemented.
Systematic image perturbationsOctober 2026 iScience issue.Separates global outline, internal parts and texture across over 200 networks, using 240 manually isolated exemplars. It motivates diagnostic ablations, not a recognition algorithm or one-image performance guarantee.
Optimal Partial Graph MatchingICLR 2025; first preprint October 2024.Unmatched-node penalties reduce to assignment with dummy choices. The optimization needs no pretrained weights; published visual experiments use pretrained VGG16 and trained graph embeddings. Only the assignment idea is adopted.
Cost-Regularized Unbalanced OTNovember 2025 preprint.Alternates transport and bounded cost adaptation; demonstrations include single-cell multiomics. Query-specific metric fitting could make unrelated pairs align. Not implemented; stationarity results do not establish semantic correctness.
Iterative Optimal Transport2026 journal volume; online November 2025.Training-free edge-point registration combines partial mass with penalized polynomial deformation. Same-anatomy multimodal registration differs from category recognition. Flexible geometric refinement is deferred.
Partial Gromov–Wasserstein MetricICLR 2025; first preprint February 2024.Quadratic transport compares internal distances while allowing missing mass. Solver mathematics needs no pretraining; some classification experiments train an SVM. Not implemented: identical tile grids contain no object-specific geometry.
Fused Partial Gromov–WassersteinFirst preprint February 2025; September revision.Combines appearance, pairwise structural discrepancy and partial mass. It accepts supplied graph features; graph experiments do not prove one-image photo recognition. Deferred pending meaningful contour or feature graphs.
Robust Alignment via Partial GWJune 2025 preprint.Statistical robustness requires moment and contamination assumptions; its stated breakdown occurs at contamination one third. It does not justify HyperNet’s 40% unmatched allowance or guarantee foreground recovery.
Supervised GW with Metric-Preserving ConstraintsSIAM journal publication, February 2025.Pairwise incompatibility constraints encode metric preservation. Demonstrations include synthetic and single-cell data; “supervised” need not mean pretrained vision. HyperNet’s unary displacement penalty does not reproduce those constraints.
Tree-Sliced Entropy Partial TransportNeurIPS 2025.Subtree mass differences enable efficient partial distances. Some image-generation experiments use pretrained autoencoders and many images. Deferred: exact assignment is already inexpensive for four or nine parts.

Originality and practical limits

The defensible contribution is an experimentally evaluated combination under a strict reference-learning budget. Gaussian simulation, graph cuts, covariance weighting, assignment and partial transport are prior art. A regular coordinate grid describes layout, not object anatomy; its penalty neither verifies a homography nor measures pairwise contour geometry. Stable backgrounds can still dominate, automatic foreground seeds can be wrong, and shuffles can penalize legitimate symmetric objects.

One training image per reference does not mean one image informed the entire research process: development photographs used to select methods or thresholds are method-design data and must be counted separately. Fresh evaluation must preserve that separation. The experiment can establish whether these specific changes help; it cannot by itself establish human-like reasoning, general category invariance or a new transport theorem.
