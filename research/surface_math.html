Transfer still needs evidence.

The v10 experiment uses established geometry to train our own CNN from random weights. Its eight families are explicitly supervised. A new object program from a familiar family is a useful holdout, but it is not an unseen natural category. None of the graphics sources below reports the one-image natural-photo recognition claim HyperNet ultimately seeks.

Sources, dates and actual adoption

Barr, January 1981: signed-power superquadrics give compact control over rounded, boxlike and pinched forms. The current superellipsoid and supertoroid use this established family. With Ce(t)=sign(cos t)|cos t|e and corresponding Se, a superellipsoid is (aCe1(u)Ce2(v), bCe1(u)Se2(v), cSe1(u)). The paper also discusses normals and curvature-dependent sampling. This is prior geometry, not our novelty. Original publication record.

Gielis, 1 March 2003: the superformula controls contour symmetry and exponents. It is a possible broader contour generator, but the current radial rosette uses its own explicit harmonic definition and should not be labeled a Gielis implementation. Closure must be checked for chosen parameters. No learned model or recognition budget appears in this geometry paper. Primary full text.

Perlin, 2002: improved gradient noise replaces the cubic fade with 6t5−15t4+10t3, making endpoint first and second derivatives vanish. It is a candidate for smooth multiscale displacement, not an ingredient in the frozen renderer. The paper studies graphics quality and computation, not category learning. Author's paper.

Worley, 1996: nearest-feature-point distance functions generate cellular patterns. They could broaden the surface curriculum beyond bumps and grooves. F2−F1 is a useful boundary cue, not exact boundary distance. This is also a future alternative rather than an adopted v10 component, and has no image-recognition training budget. What the frozen renderer actually computes

The eight names identify explicit generating programs: superellipsoid, supertoroid, lobed vase, trefoil tube, corrugated torus, saddle shell, radial rosette and multipart spindle. Some are familiar mathematical surfaces; others are engineered combinations of lathes, harmonics and components. The trefoil follows a (2,3) torus-knot centerline. A radial-projection tube frame is used; it is not parallel transport. The exact expressions and references are recorded separately in the renderer notes.

g(u,v) = sum_j a_j exp(−0.5[(du_j/su_j)^2 + (dv_j/sv_j)^2]) / sum_j |a_j|
f(u,v) = 0.8 g(u,v) + 0.2 periodic_grooves(u,v)
X_strength(u,v) = X_base(u,v) + strength · f(u,v) · n_base(u,v)

There are six Gaussian bumps and bounded periodic grooves. With |g|≤1 and groove magnitude≤1, displacement magnitude is bounded by strength before fixed base-object normalization. Strength is 0, 0.03 or 0.07. Normals are recomputed after displacement. These Gaussians live in the parameter chart: equal parameter displacement need not correspond to equal physical distance on the object. This is neither a geodesic Gaussian field nor a learned distribution of natural category variation.

The same object's three strengths share camera, material and lighting, with the normalization fixed from its base geometry. This prevents a per-strength camera fit from impersonating a shape change. The two views vary these nuisances independently. Rendering uses orthographic projection and pixel-center triangle rasterization with interpolated normals, depth and a z-buffer. No image blur or rescaling is applied to native frames. Absence of antialiasing can still create sampling artifacts; sharp pixels are not unlimited physical detail.

Why two heads help frame the question

Category recognition asks for invariance to irrelevant differences. Surface matching asks for sensitivity to some of those same differences. A single representation may suppress useful detail when the training objective rewards broad invariance. v10 gives shape and detail separate 64-dimensional heads, then concatenates them with fixed squared weights 0.75 and 0.25. The detail path branches at a higher spatial resolution, although it still uses pooling and compression.

Shape receives supervised family cross-entropy and paired-view instance contrastive learning. Other objects within a family become contrastive negatives, creating a real tension with the family labels. Detail predicts a 64-bin masked, windowed log-power spectrum. Mask, normal and depth losses act on shared features. Spectrum bins include object boundaries and lighting effects, and are not pure surface microgeometry. Training against a renderer-derived target avoids a learned teacher, but still embeds substantial human-designed priors.

What can be claimed as original

The independently implemented generator, explicit data-budget accounting, dual-head loss combination and matched native-versus-lowpass experiment form an engineering prototype. The mathematics of superquadrics, Gaussian basis functions, power spectra, neural convolutions and contrastive learning is established. We have not performed a comprehensive novelty search proving this exact combination is first, and the present experiment cannot establish a human-like mechanism.

A meaningful next result would preserve improvements across independent generator seeds and harder held-out structural families, then transfer to fresh natural photos using the declared single real reference. Neither a high score on supervised synthetic families nor a high auxiliary spectrum cosine substitutes for that evidence. The completed v10 report retains every control, including results that oppose the proposed resolution advantage.
